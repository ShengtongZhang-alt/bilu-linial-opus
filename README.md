# Bilu–Linial is False, but Only by a Tiny Margin

**Responsible maintainer:** Shengtong Zhang

The source drafts in [`docs/`](docs/) were generated by GPT 6 Astra and have not been
independently reviewed. The extension of Xu's counterexample to all degrees comes from a Claude
Opus 5.5 chat. The maintainer claims no credit for the mathematical ideas in either the drafts or
the extension. The formalization was carried out by Claude Opus 5.5 agents in Cursor, directed by
Shengtong Zhang. See "How this was produced" below and `automation` in
[`formalization.yaml`](formalization.yaml).

This repository is a Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of two bounds on how small the signed
adjacency norm of a graph can be made. Both are proved with no `sorry` and only the standard axioms
(`propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`).

## The theorems

A *signing* of a finite simple graph $G$ assigns a sign $\sigma(uv)\in \{-1, 1\}$ to every edge. Its
signed adjacency matrix $A_\sigma$ has $\sigma(uv)$ at $(u,v)$ for every edge and $0$ elsewhere,
including the diagonal, and $\|A_\sigma\|$ is its Euclidean operator norm, that is, the largest
absolute value of an eigenvalue.

The problem we want to study was proposed by Bilu and Linial [[BL06]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7): what is the smallest constant $s_d$ such that, for each $d$-regular graph $G$,
we can find a signing $\sigma$ such that $A_{\sigma}$ has spectral radius at most $s_d$?

Motivated by their study of Ramanujan graphs [[LPS88]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02126799), Bilu and Linial conjectured that $s_d = 2\sqrt{d - 1}$, the Alon–Boppana bound [[Alo86]](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579166), [[Nil91]](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(91)90112-F). An implication of their conjecture is that one can iteratively construct Ramanujan graphs using $2$-lifts.

Bilu and Linial originally proved that 

$$s_d = O(\sqrt{d \cdot \log^3 d})$$ 

using random signings. Ravichandran and Srivastava [[RS21]](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnz111) used the interlacing method to prove that

$$s_d \le 2\sqrt{2(d-1)}.$$ 

The leading constant has since been refined: Lin and Zhou [[LZ26]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15715) improved the coefficient $2\sqrt2$ to $(3+\sqrt5)/2$, and Jadbabaie, Saberi and Sra [[JSS26]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23855) found signings achieving $2\sqrt{2(d-1)}$ in polynomial time. But the exact leading constant was not known prior to this work.

On the other hand, Xu [[Xu26]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15591) recently disproved the original conjecture of Bilu and Linial, giving an explicit construction showing that $s_3 > 2\sqrt{2}$. This leaves the exact determination of $s_d$ as a tantalizing open question.

The purpose of this repository is to give a formal proof that $s_d$ is strictly greater than $2\sqrt{d - 1}$ for every $d \geq 3$, but only by a tiny margin. If we let

$$\epsilon_d := s_d - 2\sqrt{d - 1},$$

then we show that $\epsilon_d$ is polynomially small in $d$: for all sufficiently large $d$,

$$d^{-11/2} \ll \epsilon_d \ll d^{-1/2-2/17}.$$

(The tight draft also claims the sharper lower bound $\epsilon_d \ge (2-o(1))\,d^{-5/2}$, through the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick constant and random regular graphs; that bound is not formalized here.)

More precisely, we prove the following two results, establishing the upper and lower bounds respectively.

**Near-Ramanujan signings** (`BiluLinial.near_ramanujan_signing`). There are absolute constants
$C$ and $d_0$ such that, for every integer $d\ge d_0$, every finite simple graph of maximum degree
at most $d$ has a signing with

$$
\|A_\sigma\| < \sqrt{4(d-1) + C\,d^{-2/17}} = 2\sqrt{d-1} + O\bigl(d^{-1/2-2/17}\bigr).
$$

$C$ and $d_0$ do not depend on the graph, in particular not on its number of vertices. This is
the upper bound of Theorem 1 of [`docs/second_order_bilu_linial_tight.tex`](docs/second_order_bilu_linial_tight.tex).

**An explicit excess** (`BiluLinial.explicit_excess`). For every integer $d\ge4$ there is a finite,
connected, $d$-regular graph all of whose signings satisfy

$$
\|A_\sigma\| \ge 2\sqrt{d-1} + \frac{(d-1)^{-11/2}}{25}.
$$

This is Section 6 of [`docs/second_order_bilu_linial.tex`](docs/second_order_bilu_linial.tex)
with an explicit constant. For an algebraic number $R_q = 2\sqrt q + (1+O(1/q))\,q^{-11/2}$,
$q=d-1$, and every threshold $R<R_q$, the draft constructs such a graph (depending on $R$) whose
signings all satisfy $\|A_\sigma\|\ge R$. The constant $1/25$ is ours, and the draft's form is
proved too (`BiluLinial.SecondOrder.Explicit.explicit_excess_proof`).

**Library theorem, not compared** (`BiluLinial.bilu_linial_counterexample_proof` in
`BiluLinial/Main.lean`). For every $d\ge3$ there is a finite, connected, $d$-regular graph all of
whose signings have $\|A_\sigma\|>2\sqrt{d-1}$. This extends Xu's cubic counterexample
([`docs/COUNTEREXAMPLE_ALL_DEGREES.md`](docs/COUNTEREXAMPLE_ALL_DEGREES.md)). For $d\ge4$ it
follows from `explicit_excess`; it is kept because it also covers $d=3$.

## Comparison with prior work

We emphasize that our work depends heavily on recent work in the area: the lower bound is a generalization of [[Xu26]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15591), and the upper bound builds upon [[JSS26]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23855).

Bilu and Linial [[BL06]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-006-0029-7) conjectured that every
$d$-regular graph has a signing with $\|A_\sigma\|\le2\sqrt{d-1}$ and proved
$O(\sqrt{d\log^3d})$. Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava
[[MSS15]](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2015.182.1.7) proved the one-sided bound
$\lambda_{\max}(A_\sigma)\le2\sqrt{d-1}$, which settles bipartite graphs.

The compared theorems show that $2\sqrt{d-1}$ is correct to second order but is never exact for
$d\ge4$. Both come from unreviewed drafts. A brief web search found no earlier Lean formalization
of bounds on Bilu–Linial signings (novelty beyond that search is unknown); the closest Lean work
we know of is [godsil-gutman-lean](https://github.com/karlesmarin/godsil-gutman-lean)
(the Godsil–Gutman identity and the Bilu–Linial 2-lift factorization), which this development
neither uses nor overlaps.

## Scope and differences from the sources

[`FORMALIZATION.md`](FORMALIZATION.md) gives the full source-to-Lean correspondence. In short:

- `near_ramanujan_signing` writes the draft's $q^{-2/17}$ ($q=d-1$) as $d^{-2/17}$ and its $\le$ as
  $<$. Because $C$ is existential, these forms are equivalent, and the equivalences are proved in
  [`BiluLinial/SanityD.lean`](BiluLinial/SanityD.lean). The same file proves that the radius is
  $2\sqrt{d-1}+O(d^{-1/2-2/17})$, that $C\le0$ is impossible (by `explicit_excess`), and that the
  statement implies the earlier upper bound $2\sqrt d+O(d^{1/6})$.
- `explicit_excess` uses the explicit constant $1/25$; it is implied by the draft's sharper form,
  which is also proved.
- Not formalized: the lower bound of Theorem 1 of the tight draft (Section 2, via the
  Sherrington–Kirkpatrick constant and random regular graphs) and Section 7 of the second-order
  draft.
- The proof of the upper bound follows Section 1 of the tight draft with these changes. Its one
  use of the Gaussian correlation inequality (Lemma "Uniform incident-row gain", in the form of
  [Mil25]) is replaced by a different estimate. The deletion lemma's ODE lift becomes a
  Knaster–Tarski fixed point. The Brascamp–Lieb step is proved from the midpoint Prékopa–Leindler
  inequality. Several local gaps of the draft were repaired. None of these changes affects the
  statement; [`docs/BLUEPRINT.md`](docs/BLUEPRINT.md) records each one.
- The earlier upper bounds $(2+o(1))\sqrt d$ and $(2+O(\log\log d/\log d))\sqrt d$ of
  [`docs/asymptotic_bilu_linial.tex`](docs/asymptotic_bilu_linial.tex), and $2\sqrt d+O(d^{1/6})$
  of Theorem 1.1 of `docs/second_order_bilu_linial.tex`, were formalized here first and deleted
  once `near_ramanujan_signing` (which implies them) was proved; they are not included here.

## Outline of the Lean proofs

**Upper bound** (`BiluLinial/Tight/`, node by node in `docs/BLUEPRINT.md`, Part D). Put $q=d-1$,
$p=\lfloor c_0 d^{2/17}\rfloor$, $R^2=4q+4/p$ and $a=1/R$. For vertex sources $y^\pm\in[0,s]^V$, the
normalized precision matrices are $P^\pm=\operatorname{diag}D(y^\pm)\pm a\,Y^{1/2}A_\sigma Y^{1/2}$.
Each signing gets the weight $\mathbf 1\{P^+\succ0,P^-\succ0\}(\det P^+\det P^-)^p$, and $Z$ is the
total weight.

1. *Extraction* (`Tight/Extract.lean`). If $Z>0$ at $y^\pm=s\mathbf 1$ on the whole graph, some
   signing has $\|A_\sigma\|<R$.
2. *Induction* (`Tight/Core.lean`, `Tight/Step.lean`). By strong induction on vertex sets $S$:
   for all sources in the box, $Z>0$, and the mean diagonal resolvents $\mathbb E\,h_i^\pm$ stay
   below the cap $r=1+\eta_0/2$.
   - Positivity uses a Schur-complement insertion identity, a deletion lemma (a monotone
     fixed-point construction) and a floor estimate.
   - The cap is kept by an expanding-cube first-contact argument. A first contact with the cap is
     ruled out by `exists_contact_free` (`Tight/Contact.lean`), the formal version of the draft's
     Sections 1.2–1.5: endpoint comparison and concentration, shifted means, fresh-star row
     energies, and the contact estimate.
3. *Analytic tools*, proved in finite dimension:
   - the midpoint Prékopa–Leindler inequality and Prékopa's theorem;
   - covariance bounds for even log-concave perturbations of a Gaussian, and a Brascamp–Lieb
     inequality for linear functions (`Tight/Gauss/`);
   - Gaussian integration by parts (`Tight/Gauss/`, `Tight/Tools/`) and Rademacher-to-Gaussian
     comparisons (`Tight/Compare/`).

   The library also proves Royen's Gaussian correlation monotonicity for two symmetric polyhedra
   and for two centred ellipsoids (`Tight/Gauss/GCI*.lean`, following [LM17]); the final proof does
   not use it.
4. *Constants.* Every "absolute constant", $o(1)$ and "sufficiently large $d$" of the draft is an
   existential, chosen in this order: absolute constants, then $\kappa_0$, then $c_0$, then the
   degree threshold. All of them are uniform in the graph, and $C=8/c_0$.

**Lower bound** (`BiluLinial/SecondOrder/Explicit/`, Part C2 of `docs/BLUEPRINT.md`).
1. The graph is built from a triangle seed, $(d-1)$-ary trees, iterated joins and a centre, then
   completed to a $d$-regular graph. Schur complements turn the norm bound into scalar recursions
   for rooted Green's functions.
2. The threshold is $R=qt+1/t$ with $t=(1-1/(5q^3))/\sqrt q$; one checks directly that the
   draft's polynomial $F_q(t^2)$ is positive there.

- **Direction.** The maintainer supplied the drafts and chose which results to target and their
  final form. A supervising Claude Opus 5.5 session in Cursor set up the repository and launched
  a coordinator agent of the same model with `cursor-agent`.
- **Formalization.** The coordinator wrote the statements, the sanity lemmas and
  `FORMALIZATION.md`, and broke each theorem into a lemma DAG (`docs/BLUEPRINT.md`). Separate audit
  passes went over the Part D draft (`docs/tight/`). Before a node went to a prover, its statement
  was checked: a proof sketch, the parent proved from it, and small cases. Up to ten subagents
  worked in parallel, 120 subagent tasks in all, under the rules in `AGENTS.md` and
  `.cursor/rules/`. The work ran from 2026-09-29 19:41 to 2026-10-01 05:47 (UTC−7).
- **Packaging.** A Claude Opus 5.5 agent prepared the Palomar submission on 2026-10-01: the
  module-system port, the metadata, the scripts and CI. It changed no proof content apart from one
  `set_option maxHeartbeats` on a single lemma.

The maintainer has reviewed `Challenge.lean` and is convinced that it formalizes the results
correctly, but has not reviewed the other formalization files or the source drafts. The Lean kernel
and the independent kernels run by Comparator check the proofs. Whether the statements say what the
drafts claim is also addressed by `FORMALIZATION.md` and the sanity lemmas.
